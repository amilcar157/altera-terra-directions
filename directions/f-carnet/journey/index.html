Field Notes — Kyoto, Japan

Kyoto, in the quiet season

The Brief

Composed for a family of five — two generations — who wanted Kyoto without the queues, and without each other's itineraries getting in the way.

Composed to order — there is no price list, only the shape of what a family like this one needs.

  • Experiences

Five Moments

How the five days were composed

  1. 01

    A ryokan, held in full

    The whole house, not a wing of it — so three bedrooms could have their doors open to each other, or not, as the week required.

  2. 02

    The garden before the gates open

    A private hour inside a moss garden most visitors only see through a crowd, arranged with the temple directly, not around it.

  3. 03

    A potter, not a demonstration

    An afternoon in a working studio in Shigaraki, with a craftsman who agreed to one visit, not a standing class.

  4. 04

    Dinner at a counter of seven seats

    A kaiseki room usually booked a season out, held for this one evening by a relationship, not a reservation line.

  5. 05

    The last morning, deliberately empty

    No plan at all — because the family had asked, on the second day, for one morning with nowhere to be.
